this garden is a culmination of all of my **everyday thoughts**. i have a general rule of if i think about it twice, i write it down because that means it was important enough to me for me to dedicate more than one ounce of brain energy to it.

you may be wondering, or you may not, if i'm enjoying writing and sharing my thoughts so much: why a garden and not a blog. and to that, i say it's down to the core philosophy of digital gardens.

in that you're prioritizing the ==topography of the site over the timeline==. a feed gives you a timeline, it gives you structure, but it hides older notes in a way that's not intuitive to the way that our brains work.

and so a garden really allows you to make a **mind map** and go back and re-explore old thoughts in a way that is much more accessible than if you were trying to do it on a time-based feed.

here's the honest inventory, more or less in the order that i tend to it:

- general thought processes
- love and acceptance
- philosophical findings (im being pretentious, let me have this)
- me! the author!
- you! if you want!

the house rules, such as they are:

- **everything has meaning** even if you don't believe in it
- this is an inherently ==political== space due to the nature of the author
- this is not however a place for bigotry of any kind. and if you find that your idea of politics is arguing wether or not people deserve basic human rights (food, water, shelter, life, liberty, and the ability to pursue happiness), then this is not a safe space for you
- when i say politics i mean things like how we allocate our taxes, what we prioritize teaching our children, and wether or not that art piece makes a statement or is simply a banana taped to the wall.
- learning with the **garage door up** is essential to actually learning
- opinions are essential
- irrefutable fact is to be respected
- and always **question everything**

two honest paths through, pick whichever feels more like you:

- the random path: hit the random button until you see something that interests you. follow the links on the page until you discover something that makes you go "huh. neat" or "what is this dude on about". stop and smell the roses. repeat.
- the index path: go to the index button and navigate through the tabs like chapters in a book. that is also valid. if you think of a secret third way to go through the garden, by all means please do, and if you have the time and want, please share with the rest of us.

hell yeah, i'm glad you'd like to. you are more than welcome to do any or all of the following:

- go see how other people host their gardens! i'll link a few of my faves here: [maggie appleton](https://maggieappleton.com/), [andy matsuchak](https://notes.andymatuschak.org/About_these_notes), [paul kenney](https://paulkenny.neocities.org/pages/Digital%20Garden), [hana](https://wildflowergarden.nekoweb.org/), [jacky zhao](https://jzhao.xyz/)
- make a fork (or copy) of my repository (the files that make up my site)
- ask me about my process with making the site (i also have a comprehensive guide/course coming up on my other site (which is totally free and super cool btw))
- send your recommendations of what you think i should look into. if there is something you want me to write about, send it my way!!!

the most important thing to remember is to have fun with it. if you spend too much time trying to optimize everything to be perfect, you will run out of steam before you even start writing.
